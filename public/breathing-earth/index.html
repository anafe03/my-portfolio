How it works

Every colour on land is a satellite measurement. Green is NDVI, the vegetation index: leaves reflect near-infrared light and absorb red, so the ratio between the two says how much living, leafy growth a place has. Bare sand reads near 0, a rainforest near 0.9. White is the share of the month a place was under snow.

The northern hemisphere holds most of the land, so when its spring arrives the whole planet greens and the snow retreats: an inhale. Six months later the south takes its turn while the north browns and whitens.

Then · Now compares the same calendar month averaged over 2001–2003 against 2023–2025 (snow: 2003–2005, when Aqua's record starts). Change paints the difference: greener now, browner now, less snow, more snow. Three-year averages soften one-off years, but the differences still include cloud gaps, sensor ageing and processing changes, so read them as a picture, not a measurement.
