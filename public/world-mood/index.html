How it works

Every 15 minutes The GDELT Project publishes the news events its machines read in that window, from outlets worldwide in more than 100 languages. This page pulls those files straight into your browser.

Tone is how the coverage reads, not a judgment of the country. It's the average sentiment of each article's text, roughly −10 to +10. A country's color is the tone of stories about events located there in the newest window, weighted by how widely each was picked up, and pulled toward its usual tone when there are only a few stories.

News runs negative, about −1.5 on a typical day, so colors center there: violet reads darker than usual news, amber brighter. Glow is how much is being written. Shift colors each country against its own last 24 hours instead.

Breaking means a country's coverage spiked well above its usual 15-minute volume, or turned sharply darker, in the newest window. The globe turns to face it.

Headlines are rebuilt from article links, so the wording can be rough, and places come from automated geocoding that is sometimes wrong. Map: Natural Earth via world-atlas.
