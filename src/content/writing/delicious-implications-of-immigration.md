Immigration has been a contentious issue in the US for as long as I've been alive. Go to a bar or a dinner party and it's quite common to hear people arguing about borders, economics, demographics, privilege, or whether the country is falling apart.

I'm not gonna talk about any of that.

I wanna talk about something much more interesting.

Food.

Cultural diffusion has been changing food for centuries.

Before 1492, Italian food had no tomatoes.

No chicken parm? No red sauce? No spaghetti?

I thought they were serving that to Caesar. Chef Boyardee lied to me.

Thai food didn't have chilis either. Those came from the Americas too.

Okay, those are pretty iconic parts of both cuisines now.

That's what's so interesting. Things move around and people borrow ideas from each other. They change them, and given enough time people forget where they came from.

I'm a big fan of salmon nigiri.

You know salmon sushi is actually pretty new in Japan? Norway spent years trying to convince the Japanese to eat Atlantic salmon raw, and now it's one of the most popular sushi varieties in the world.

I love American Christmas food too, even though sometimes it just feels like Thanksgiving leftovers.

You know what people eat for Christmas in Japan?

Kurisumasu ni wa Kentakkii.

Kentucky for Christmas.

KFC.

Fried chicken from Kentucky became a Japanese Christmas tradition.

That's cool.

You ever had a bánh mì?

That shit is bussing.

The French brought baguettes to Vietnam through colonialism. Vietnamese people took the bread, added local ingredients, and now we have a delicious sandwich.

Ketchup comes the other way. Its ancestors were fermented sauces from Asia, including fish-based sauces. Then Europeans started messing with it, changing ingredients, and eventually we ended up with the red tomato stuff we eat with french fries.

Nothing stays the same. Nothing is fixed.

Food doesn't.

Music doesn't.

Language doesn't.

Ideas don't.

And people don't.

Everything changes.

None of these things are the same as where they came from, but that's the point. They mix, they mutate, and eventually they become traditional again.

Growing up in Indiana, I always heard America was a melting pot.

That was supposed to be one of the cool things about this country.

When did that become something we were scared of?

I'm not saying every consequence of immigration is good.

But scapegoating people is a dangerous road, and pretending cultures have ever existed in a vacuum is just silly and historically inaccurate.

Whether or not the American Dream is still alive, at least the food keeps getting better.

I'm gonna go eat a bulgogi burrito and listen to the blues.

The history can be dark, but the food is delicious.
