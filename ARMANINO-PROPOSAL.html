An AI Playbook for Armanino Valuation Services

Three patterns in the work around the valuation — and a six-week pilot to prove one out.
Prepared for Armanino — Business Valuation Services  ·  July 2026

The judgment in a valuation — methodology, inputs, defensibility — is your team's, and stays that way. But that judgment is wrapped in a great deal of repeatable document and data labor: re-keying facts, drafting boilerplate, screening lists, tying out numbers, chasing intake. That labor is where AI helps. Below are three recurring shapes of it, each with one worked example.

The pattern is the asset. Solve a shape once for 409A and the same machinery serves PPA, goodwill, and ESOP work at a fraction of the cost — inside this practice, on this P&L.

The three patterns

01

Structured extraction — facts that exist in prose, re-keyed by hand before anything can use them.

Cap table → OPM inputs · 409A. Today an analyst reads the charter and every SPA and types the preference stack into the model by hand — hours per deal, and a transcription error moves the number. Instead: parse the docs → extract each term to a fixed schema with the document, page, and quoted span → the analyst reviews only low-confidence rows. The OPM references the tab; nothing writes into your model.
InputCharter + SPAs
→
ModelExtract to schema
→
OutputFacts tab · term · page · quote
→
HumanReviews low-confidence rows
↩ Every override feeds back — it becomes the accuracy number, and trains the model to stop asking about the rows the human keeps fixing.
Measured by field accuracy vs an analyst baseline on ten past deals. Never chooses inputs or touches a model cell. Reuses on PPA, ESOP, and litigation deal files.
02

Grounded drafting — prose that is mostly pattern and a little fact, written from a blank page every time.

RFP responses & engagement proposals. Today, written from scratch or salvaged from an old one each time — slow turnaround costs deals. Instead: retrieval over your library of past proposals, credentials, and case studies → a tailored draft in your template and voice → every claim cited to a real past engagement → BD reviews and sends.
InputPast proposals + credentials
→
ModelDraft, cited
→
OutputTailored draft · every claim cited
→
HumanBD reviews & sends
↩ Every edit feeds back — the sent version becomes a new example, so the next draft starts closer to what you'd actually send.
Measured by hours to first draft; share of text surviving review; uncited claims (target zero). Never chooses the sources or writes the conclusion. Reuses on repeatable report sections — company description, methodology narrative.
03

Compliance & consistency checking — read a document against the rules it must follow, mark the evidence, and suggest the fix for a person to review.

Compliance review · 409A / ASC 718. Today a human reads a hundred pages against the standards and the model, hunting for the required element that's missing and the number that didn't get updated — either one becomes the auditor's finding. Instead: check the draft against each requirement → mark it met or missing, with the exact supporting text quoted, and suggest language for the gaps → tie every narrative number back to its model cell and flag what doesn't reconcile → hand the reviewer a marked-up exception list, each item citing the rule or the cell. A person accepts, edits, or rejects every item.
InputDraft + 409A/ASC 718 rules
→
ModelCheck & mark evidence
→
OutputException list · gap + suggested fix
→
HumanAccepts / edits / rejects
↩ Every accept and reject feeds back — each becomes a labeled check, so the review gets sharper on the gaps your reviewers actually catch.
Measured by defects caught before review vs after; review cycles per report. Never edits the report — it suggests, a human decides.

The model occupies one box. It reads and drafts — it never computes, and never writes anywhere a person can't see what it changed and why. Every reviewer override becomes the accuracy number, built from work your team already does.
Built the way I build regulated agents: retrieval with permissions, typed extraction schemas, a human review step before anything is trusted, and audit logging on every model call — so any output can be traced and defended.

The pilot — one pattern, six weeks, a stop condition agreed up front

One of the three patterns — we decide together, on the clearest ground truth and the highest leverage6 weeks
Weeks 1–2
Ground truth
A set of past examples with their known-good outputs; a reviewer defines "good."
Weeks 3–4
Build
Build the tool for the chosen pattern — cited and evaluated throughout.
Week 5
Bake-off
Live engagements, side by side. Reviewer marks up blind.
Week 6
Decide
Go, adjust, or stop — on the numbers.
Success is defined on day one: hours to first draft, share of text surviving review, zero uncited factual claims. If it misses, we say so and stop.

To scope this, I'd want to understand

1
Where your information lives, and how it moves
Where documents and data sit today, how you track status across an engagement, and how things get ingested now — plus what can leave the tenant and who signs off on that.
2
A few examples for ground truth
Real input-and-output pairs to build against — past RFPs and the responses that were sent, a charter + SPAs + cap table alongside the OPM inputs they produced, or past reports with the reviewer's markups. The known-good output is what makes accuracy measurable.
3
Someone close to the work to define "good"
A few hours across the pilot from a person who does this daily. Their judgment is the target; their corrections become the accuracy number.
